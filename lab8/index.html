THE STUDY

Compare all three methods before choosing a winner.

A research team randomly assigns 45 participants to one of three training methods, with 15 participants per method. Standard Training uses instruction and independent practice. Guided Training adds worked examples and coached practice. Interactive Training adds guided practice and immediate feedback. Each participant completes one standardized performance task after training. Scores range from 0–100; higher is better. Each person belongs to only one group.

This dataset is synthetic and designed for learning.

Do population mean performance scores differ among the three training methods?

Analyze in jamovi Cloud

Use the original CSV; the later simulation is a separate investigation.

↓ Download lab dataCSV · original analysis dataset

  1. Open jamovi Cloud in its own tab. Follow the sign-in prompts and use free Guest access. No additional modules are required; free access may have queues and session limits.
  2. Download Lab8_Training_Data.csv. In Cloud’s file menu choose Open, then the option to upload or browse for a file on your device. Select the saved CSV. Labels may vary.
  3. Check the columns using a heading’s setup panel. Participant ID → Text / ID; Training Method → Text / Nominal; Performance Score → Integer or Decimal / Continuous. Leave Participant ID out of the analysis.
  4. Choose Analyses → ANOVA → One-Way ANOVA. Move Performance Score to Dependent Variables and Training Method to the grouping-variable box.
  5. For the primary analysis, select Fisher’s (equal variances assumed). This is the conventional one-way ANOVA. Welch’s may be selected by default; record Fisher’s output for this lab.
  6. Request Descriptives and a descriptive plot. Under assumption checks, request the homogeneity test (Levene’s) and a Q–Q plot if available. Inspect unusual scores and spread, rather than treating an assumption-test p value as proof.
  7. Under Post Hoc Tests, choose Tukey (equal variances assumed). Record the mean differences and Tukey-adjusted p values for all three pairs. Interpret them alongside the omnibus result.
  8. Use F and its two degrees of freedom to calculate η² with the formula in Part 7. The simplified One-Way ANOVA menu may not display η² directly.
  9. Capture the descriptives, Fisher ANOVA, and Tukey tables. Note the group subtraction order in each comparison.
  10. Download a saved .omv analysis after changes and before leaving Cloud. Save your Word report separately. Insert readable screenshots of the requested output.
Assumptions and jamovi help

Fisher’s ANOVA assumes independent observations, approximately normal within-group errors, and equal population variances. Independence follows from the design. Welch’s ANOVA with Games–Howell comparisons is an alternative when equal variances are not reasonable; do not mix its results with the required Fisher/Tukey analysis.

PART 06 · WORD REPORT

Make the decision

Use α = .05 and distinguish the statistical decision from its practical meaning.

  1. Reject or fail to reject the overall H₀ at α = .05? Cite F, both degrees of freedom, and p.
  2. Use Tukey-adjusted p values to identify supported pairwise differences. Name the direction and the subtraction order for each mean difference.
  3. Can you identify a uniquely best method from these results? Distinguish the highest sample mean from evidence that it exceeds each alternative. Compare with your prediction.
Decision wording

Reject H₀ when p < .05; otherwise fail to reject it. A failure to reject does not establish the null as true. A p value is not the probability that the null is true.

PART 07 · WORD REPORT

Beyond statistical significance

Connect the evidence to a defensible conclusion.

  1. Calculate η² = (F × df_between) / (F × df_between + df_within) from the Fisher ANOVA. Interpret the proportion of observed score variation associated with training group.
  2. Critique: “The ANOVA is significant, so all three methods differ and the method with the highest mean must be best.” Use both omnibus and Tukey results.
  3. What additional information about costs, time, implementation, and generalizability would guide adoption? Explain what random assignment supports and what it does not establish about other settings.
Why does this matter?

The omnibus ANOVA asks whether all population means are equal. A significant result means at least one difference exists; it does not name that difference. Tukey adjusts for examining all three pairs. A nonsignificant pairwise result does not prove equal population means.

How the simulation works

Grand mean = average of the three means. SS_between = n × Σ(Mgroup − grand mean)². SS_within = 3(n − 1)s². F = (SS_between / 2) / s². df = 2 and 3(n − 1). η² = SS_between / (SS_between + SS_within). The p value uses the F distribution.

  1. Reset and record the baseline. Increase only shared within-group SD. Explain changes in F, p, η², and the decision.
  2. Reset, then increase only n per group. Record a setting and explain why the evidence can change while the three means stay fixed.
  3. Reset, then move the Interactive mean toward the other means. Record a setting and explain how between-group separation affects F.
