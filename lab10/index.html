THE STUDY

A predicted score is an estimate, not a guarantee.

Use the same observational practice-hours and performance dataset introduced in Lab 9. Thirty volunteers reported weekly practice time and completed a 0–100 performance task. Practice hours were not assigned. Here the question changes: use practice hours as X to predict performance score as Y. Fit one straight-line model to these 30 observations; there is no separate validation sample.

This dataset is synthetic and designed for learning.

Does weekly practice time help predict performance score with a linear model, and what are the limits of its predictions?

Assumptions and jamovi help

The straight-line model assumes an appropriate linear mean relationship, independent errors, and roughly constant error variance. Normal errors support the usual small-sample tests and intervals. Residual plots help assess these assumptions; independence comes from study design. A prediction beyond the observed X range is extrapolation.

PART 06 · WORD REPORT

Make the decision

Use α = .05 and distinguish the statistical decision from its practical meaning.

  1. At α = .05, reject or fail to reject H₀ for the slope? Cite b₁, its t, df, and p. Interpret its 95% CI in score points per hour.
  2. Write your fitted equation using unstandardized coefficients. Predict performance at X = 6 hours and show the substitution.
  3. For P05, calculate predicted performance and the residual Y − ŷ. Interpret the sign. Then calculate the predicted score at X = 15 and explain why that prediction deserves extra caution.
Decision wording

Reject H₀ when p < .05; otherwise fail to reject it. A failure to reject does not establish the null as true. A p value is not the probability that the null is true.

PART 07 · WORD REPORT

Beyond statistical significance

Connect the evidence to a defensible conclusion.

  1. Interpret R² as the proportion of observed outcome variation accounted for by this fitted linear model. Explain why it is not prediction accuracy for new individuals.
  2. Inspect the residual plots and Q–Q plot. Describe any pattern or unusual observation, and explain what would concern you about linearity, constant error spread, or inference.
  3. Critique: “The slope is significant, so the model predicts everyone accurately and adding an hour of practice causes the predicted gain.” Discuss uncertainty, omitted influences, causation, and the need to evaluate new data.
Why does this matter?

A residual is observed Y minus predicted ŷ. The slope is a predicted mean-score difference per extra hour, not a guaranteed individual gain. A confidence interval for the mean response and a prediction interval for one new person are different; the latter is wider because individuals vary around the line.

How the simulation works

ŷ = 55 + b₁X. The spread control sets s = √[SSE/(n − 2)], the residual standard error. SE(b₁) = s/√Sxx; t = b₁/SE(b₁); df = n − 2. The 95% interval for one new observation is ŷ ± t*.975 × s × √[1 + 1/n + (X − Mx)²/Sxx]. It assumes the fitted model continues to hold and does not protect against extrapolation errors.

  3. Reset. Move the prediction input from 6 to 15 hours, keeping the fitted model fixed. Explain the extrapolation warning and why the 95% prediction interval is not a guarantee.
